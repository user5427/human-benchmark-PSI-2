\section{System and bussiness overview}
\label{sec:system_overview}

This section provides an overview of the Human Benchmark system and its business logic. Various analysis methods, including SWOT, MOST, and SMART, will be used to analyze the system and its features.

\subsection{VISION analysis}
People can process information at least 10\% faster, which can greatly benefit them in daily life.

\subsection{MISSION analysis}
Provide an engaging and competitive app for users to measure, track, and improve their reaction speed. 

\subsection{SWOT analysis}
SWOT analysis is a tool used to identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats in business planning. This section provides an analysis of these aspects in relation to our system.

\subsubsection{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enhance cognitive function}: Faster reaction times (RTs) are linked to improved cognitive functions like memory, verbal fluency, and processing speed. Studies, including those on medical students, show that quicker RTs are associated with higher academic performance \cite{CognitiveFunction}.
    \item \textbf{Increased engagement through gamification}: Integrating game elements like leaderboards can boost motivation and engagement in learning activities. Gamification not only makes learning enjoyable but also inherently motivates students, as competition is a primary driver for game participation \cite{GameplayEngagement}.
    \item \textbf{Minimalistic design}: Research shows that minimalist design is appreciated across all generations, creating a satisfying user experience\cite{MinimalDesign}.
    \item \textbf{Increasing interest in improving reaction Time}: According to Google Trends data, search queries such as "improve reaction time" show an upward trend from 2015 to 2025 worldwide, indicating growing interest in reaction time training. 
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Weaknesses}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Technical dependence}: User experience depends on device performance, devices can affect reaction time accuracy, potentially adding 10-50ms to the measurements\cite{humanbenchmark}.
    \item \textbf{Competitive market}: The reaction training market is already competitive, with approximately 211 apps available across Google Play, the Apple App Store, and Steam.
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Opportunities}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Integration of personalized learning tools}: Research indicates that personalized learning improves both learning performance and motivation, making it a valuable approach for increasing user satisfaction\cite{PersonalizedLearning}.
    \item \textbf{Aging population}: As global demographics age, there is likely to be an increased interest in cognitive maintenance. The population aged 65 and older is projected to double by 2050 \cite{PopulationDemographics}.
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Threats}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Repetitiveness}:  Users may lose long-term interest if the app lacks regular content updates\cite{GameplayEngagement}.
    \item \textbf{Risk of overemphasis on competition}: While leaderboards can motivate some users, it may also contribute to feelings of frustration or discouragement among lower-ranked individuals, potentially reducing overall engagement.\cite{GameplayEngagement}.
    \item \textbf{Reliance on Ad Revenue}: By Q2 2023, there were 912 million active ad-blocking users worldwide, an 11\% increase from Q4 2021\cite{BlockAds}. Relying heavily on ad-based revenue can lead to inconsistent earnings, as the growing use of ad blockers reduces ad visibility, directly impacting potential revenue streams.
    
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MOST analysis}
MOST analysis is a strategic tool used to evaluate the Mission, Objectives, Strategy, and Tactics of a business or project. This section provides an analysis of these aspects in relation to our system.

\subsubsection{Mission}
\begin{itemize}
    \item To provide a reaction time training app that enhances cognitive performance.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Achieve a measurable improvement of at least 10\% in reaction time for regular users within the first 6 months.
    \item Achieve at least 10000 registered accounts within the first 6 months.
    \item Maintain a 30-day retention rate of atleast 30\% with users completing at least 3 training sessions weekly.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Strategies}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Integrate evidence-based findings and best practices into game modes to optimize cognitive training.
    \item Design user friendly app for users of different ages and skill levels.
    \item Utilize gamification techniques to ensure the app remains engaging and prevents repetition.
    \item Enable easy tracking of user progress with simple visual feedback.
    \item Promote app through online advertisements.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Tactics}
\begin{itemize} 
    \item Use a minimal color palette.
    \item Offer multiple game modes with adjustable difficulty.
    \item Implement leaderboards to promote competition and user comparison.
    \item Provide customizable game settings.
    \item Create a personalized statistics dashboard that displays average scores, best performance, and progress over time.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Porter's Methods, Base needs and additional value}

\subsubsection{Porter\'s Methods}
\begin{itemize}
 \item New competators


Since there are various ways to check one's reaction time and cognitive abilities it is relatively easy for new competators to join the market. Because of this our business needs to constantly develop and add new value to our product to make sure that we continue to be a viable rival in the market. As is mentioned in the point regarding market rivalry, there are not that many rivals as such any new-comer won't have mny rivals and their product will be, relatively, readily known among our users.

 \item Barganaing Power of Suppliers


Since our product offers all of it's services up-front, without the need for additional services for functionality, supliers have a low level of influence on our product. As such only the host of our product, the provider that offers storage of data needed for the opperation of our product and other suppliers not needed for functionality, like marketing, have power to influence our product.
 \item Substitutes

 Due to various other applications already having themselves established in the market, substitutes have a strong influence to our service since there is a large risk of users switching to another product if the alternatives have more features or provide a product of  higher quality. One example of application that could allow users to improve their reflexes via methods different to our own are video games. As such substitutes provide a strong risk of our costumers switching to a substitute, since they are far more prevalant and used than other services, simmilar to ours.

 \item Barganaing of Buyers

 Due to the alternatives available to users as mentioned above users have a strong power of barganaing as failing to follow users demands could lead them to use a different product.
 \item Market Rivalry

 Due to to the nature of our product it could be said that the service we provide is rather niche. As such the impact of other players in the market is negligable in comprison to the other driving forces. According to preliminary figures, by searching `Reaction Training` for apps (In Google play store for Android, Apple store for IOS and Steam for PC), the total result is approximately equal to 211. The acurate number can not be deduced since it's possible that duplicates have emerged among different platforms and that some apps, that have the same purpose, were excluded from this search.  But never the less, the figure obtained highlights that there are not many options to choose from as such the impact of market rivalry is negligble.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{INPUT}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Resources:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Financial:} Initial investments for salaries and server hosting.
        \item \textbf{Human Resources:} 1 Frontend Developer, 1 Backend Developer, 2 Full-Stack Developers.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User Data Collection}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Consistency:} The application counts uesers active days (at least one login and one game played). A \textbf{consistency coefficient} is calculated (resets every month):
        \begin{equation*}
            \text{Consistency coefficient} = \frac{\text{Active days this month}}{\text{Gone days this month}}
        \end{equation*}
        \item \textbf{Time Spent:} Total playtime recorded, inspired by Steam profiles (e.g., last two weeks of activity). At the start of a game a timer will start and at the end - will stop. Then it will add up on the time spent playing games today and also this week.
        \item \textbf{Popularity Metrics:} Application tracks the number of users that are logged in and playing games. Inspired by Steam Charts: active users now, peak users today (24-hour peak), all-time users peak.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Marketing \& Revenue Generation:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Marketing Company:} Third-party ad services dynamically assign ads based on user activity (e.g., Google AdSense). "We've seen a substantial revenue increase and a significant reduction in manual effort thanks to Auto ads in-page formats, while sustaining user engagement and retention. — Yangyang Xi, CEO of Lang-8, Inc."\cite{AutoAds}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{PROCESS}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Preparation}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Application/Game Development:} Programmers create/improve the application and interactive reaction-based games.
        \item \textbf{Account creation:} Users Sign up.
        \item \textbf{Personalized Ad Assignment:} User activity is tracked via cookies, and ads are assigned via third-party marketing companies.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Gaming}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Maintenance}: Bug Fixes \& Updates. Developers ensure smooth functionality and release of new games/features.
        \item \textbf{User Interaction:} Users play games and see personalized ads.
        \item \textbf{Ad Delivery \& Revenue Tracking:} The marketing company serves ads and records performance.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Data Tracking \& Analytics}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{User Progress Tracking:} The application logs reaction scores (The longer the user lasts and the higher level he reaches the more points he gets) and improvement trends (User will be able to see if he has improved - compare his scores in leaderboards and past results).
        \item \textbf{Leaderboard System:} Users are ranked based on performance over time.
        \item \textbf{Ad Analytics:} Ad views and clicks are analyzed to optimize revenue (Marketing company job).
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{OUTPUT}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Short-Term:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{User Engagement \& Satisfaction:} Measured via \textbf{Consistency Coefficient} - closer to 1 indicates higher cosistency.
        \item \textbf{Growing User Base:} Increasing active user counts and time spent on games (Better and growing popularity metrics.)
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Long-Term:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Improved Reaction Time:} Users' reaction scores should improve over time, with leaderboard rankings reflecting progress.
        \item \textbf{Ad Revenue:} Funds ongoing development and investor returns.
        \item \textbf{Potential Premium Features:} Subscription model, ad-free experience, or additional analytics for users.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\sectionnonum{Stakeholders needs}
\label{sec:stakeholders_needs}
These are all of the stakeholders needs. These needs are the foundation of our project and as such are the most important part of our project.


\begin{itemize}
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR1}{}\hyperlink{SR1}{\textbf{SR1}}: Fix the STOP button in the Reflex Test.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR2}{}\hyperlink{SR2}{\textbf{SR2}}: Adjust \textbf{game creation parameters} based on the game type (currently, all parameters are the same, including unnecessary ones).
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR3}{}\hyperlink{SR3}{\textbf{SR3}}: Optimize \textbf{screen size handling} in games (Reflex Test is too easy to play on mobile devices).
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR4}{}\hyperlink{SR4}{\textbf{SR4}}: Prevent scores from being submitted \textbf{before the game ends} (currently, random data can be entered via Swagger).
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR5}{}\hyperlink{SR5}{\textbf{SR5}}: Allow users to \textbf{change the game image}.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR6}{}\hyperlink{SR6}{\textbf{SR6}}: Implement \textbf{private games} (currently, others' created games can override yours).
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR7}{}\hyperlink{SR7}{\textbf{SR7}}: Add a \textbf{Moving Targets} game.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR8}{}\hyperlink{SR8}{\textbf{SR8}}: Enable players to \textbf{view their achievements} (high scores).
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR9}{}\hyperlink{SR9}{\textbf{SR9}}: Add a \textbf{game history} for previously played games.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR10}{}\hyperlink{SR10}{\textbf{SR10}}: Provide \textbf{more information} about the games.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR11}{}\hyperlink{SR11}{\textbf{SR11}}: Implement a \textbf{light/dark theme} toggle.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR12}{}\hyperlink{SR12}{\textbf{SR12}}: Improve \textbf{sound design and VFX} for Reflex Test and Moving Targets.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR13}{}\hyperlink{SR13}{\textbf{SR13}}: Adjust \textbf{Reflex Test scoring} based on click speed and accuracy.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR14}{}\hyperlink{SR14}{\textbf{SR14}}: Create \textbf{different Reflex Test variations}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 
        \hypertarget{SR15}{}\hyperlink{SR15}{\textbf{SR15}}: One where you play until missing a certain number of targets.
        
        \item 
        \hypertarget{SR16}{}\hyperlink{SR16}{\textbf{SR16}}: Another with a \textbf{fixed number of targets}.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR17}{}\hyperlink{SR17}{\textbf{SR17}}: Enhance the \textbf{leaderboard UI} and expand functionality for different game variations.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR18}{}\hyperlink{SR18}{\textbf{SR18}}: Allow users to view the \textbf{top 10/top 20 players} for each game separately.
    
    \item 
    \hypertarget{SR19}{}\hyperlink{SR19}{\textbf{SR19}}: Implement a \textbf{rating system} for user-created games—highly rated/popular games should be displayed higher in the list.
\end{itemize}
