\section{System and business overview}
\label{sec:system_overview}

This section provides an overview of the Human Benchmark system and its business logic. Various analysis methods, including SWOT, MOST, and SMART, will be used to analyze the system and its features.

\subsection{VISION analysis}
People can process information at least 10\% faster, which can greatly benefit them in daily life.

\subsection{MISSION analysis}
Provide an engaging and competitive app for users to measure, track, and improve their reaction speed. 

\subsection{SWOT analysis}
SWOT analysis is a tool used to identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats in business planning. This section provides an analysis of these aspects in relation to our system.

\subsubsection{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enhance cognitive function}: Faster reaction times (RTs) are linked to improved cognitive functions like memory, verbal fluency, and processing speed. Studies, including those on medical students, show that quicker RTs are associated with higher academic performance \cite{CognitiveFunction}.
    \item \textbf{Increased engagement through gamification}: Integrating game elements like leaderboards can boost motivation and engagement in learning activities. Gamification not only makes learning enjoyable but also inherently motivates students, as competition is a primary driver for game participation \cite{GameplayEngagement}.
    \item \textbf{Minimalistic design}: Research shows that minimalist design is appreciated across all generations, creating a satisfying user experience \cite{MinimalDesign}.
    \item \textbf{Increasing interest in improving reaction Time}: Search queries such as "improve reaction time" show an upward trend from 2015 to 2025 worldwide \cite{googletrends}, indicating growing interest in reaction time training. 
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Weaknesses}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Technical dependence}: User experience depends on device performance, devices can affect reaction time accuracy, potentially adding 10-50ms to the measurements\cite{humanbenchmark}.
    \item \textbf{Competitive market}: The reaction training market is already competitive, with approximately 211 apps available across Google Play, the Apple App Store, and Steam. As such it is easy for new apps to take the market.
    \item \textbf{Niche subject}: Over the last 12 months, there have been 1,955 articles associated with the term “improve reaction time”, compared to 26,212 articles related to “improve memory” \cite{muckruck}. This means that articles on reaction time improvement are published over 20 times less frequently than those on memory improvement, another aspect of cognitive performace. Fewer articles suggest that reaction time training is a niche subject, with smaller target audience.
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Opportunities}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Integration of personalized learning tools}: Research indicates that personalized learning improves both learning performance and motivation, making it a valuable approach for increasing user satisfaction\cite{PersonalizedLearning}.
    \item \textbf{Aging population}: As global demographics age, there is likely to be an increased interest in cognitive maintenance. The population aged 65 and older is projected to double by 2050 \cite{PopulationDemographics}.
    \item \textbf{Growing demand in esports \& gaming}: The esports industry is experiencing rapid growth, with the number of global esports users expected to reach 896 million by 2029 \cite{statista}. As reaction time plays a crucial role in gaming \cite{VideoGamesReaction}, there could be increasing demand for tools and apps that can help improve reaction times.

\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Threats}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Repetitiveness}:  Users may lose long-term interest if the app lacks regular content updates\cite{GameplayEngagement}.
    \item \textbf{Risk of overemphasis on competition}: While leaderboards can motivate some users, it may also contribute to feelings of frustration or discouragement among lower-ranked individuals, potentially reducing overall engagement \cite{GameplayEngagement}.
    \item \textbf{Reliance on Ad Revenue}: By Q2 2023, there were 912 million active ad-blocking users worldwide, an 11\% increase from Q4 2021 \cite{BlockAds}. Relying heavily on ad-based revenue can lead to inconsistent earnings, as the growing use of ad blockers reduces ad visibility, directly impacting potential revenue streams.
    
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MOST analysis}
MOST analysis is a strategic tool used to evaluate the Mission, Objectives, Strategy, and Tactics of a business or project. This section provides an analysis of these aspects in relation to our system.

\subsubsection{Mission}
\begin{itemize}
    \item To provide a reaction time training app that enhances cognitive performance.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Objectives}
\begin{itemize}

    \item Achieve a measurable improvement of at least 10\% in reaction time for regular users within the first 6 months. This will be tracked using \textbf{reaction scores}\ref{sec:reactionScores}.
    \item Achieve at least 10000 registered accounts within the first 6 months, indicating growth in the platform's user base.
    \item Reach an average of 500 "peak users today" (\ref{sec:steamCharts}) within the first 6 months, indicating strong daily user activity.
    \item Maintain a consistency coefficient \textbf{consistency coefficient}(\ref{sec:consistencyCoef}) of at least 0.5 across all users every month, proving user long-term retention.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Strategies}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Integrate evidence-based findings and best practices into game modes to optimize cognitive training.
    \item Design user friendly app for users of different ages and skill levels.
    \item Utilize gamification techniques to ensure the app remains engaging and prevents repetition.
    \item Enable easy tracking of user progress with simple visual feedback.
    \item Increase the app's visibility.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Tactics}
\begin{itemize} 
    \item Collaborate with cognitive scientists.
    \item Use a minimal color palette.
    \item Provide customizable app settings.
    \item Offer multiple game modes with adjustable difficulty.
    \item Implement leaderboards to promote competition and user comparison.
    \item Create a personalized statistics dashboard that displays average scores, best performance, and progress over time.
    \item Promote app through Google Ads.  
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Porter's Methods, Base needs and additional value}

\subsubsection{Porter\'s Methods}
\begin{itemize}
 \item New competators

Since developing a reaction time training app is not technologically challenging, many new competitors may enter the market. To compete with established businesses, new entrants must prioritize customer service. A customer-centric approach that delivers unique value and personalized experiences is essential for differentiation\cite{competitiveEdge}. Existing businesses must also focus on these aspects to maintain their competitive edge.
 \item Barganaing power of suppliers


Since our product provides all its services upfront without requiring additional functionality-based services, suppliers have minimal influence over it. As a result, only the product host, the essential data storage provider, and non-technical suppliers, such as marketing services, affect our product.

 \item Substitutes

 Due to various other applications already having themselves established in the market, substitutes have a strong influence to our service since there is a large risk of users switching to another product if the alternative has more features or provide a product of higher quality. One example of a substitute is action games, which, according to research, can significantly improve reaction time \cite{VideoGamesReaction}. As a result, substitutes pose a significant risk, as they are more prevalent and widely used than services similar to ours.

 \item Barganaing of buyers

Due to the available alternatives, users hold strong bargaining power. Failing to meet their demands could lead to them switching to a different product.
 
 \item Market Rivalry

 Due to to the nature of our product it could be said that the service we provide is rather niche. As such the impact of other players in the market is significant. According to preliminary figures, by searching `Reaction Training` for apps (In Google play store for Android, Apple store for IOS and Steam for PC), the total result is approximately equal to 211. The figure obtained highlights that there are many options to choose from as such the impact of market rivalry is considerable.
\end{itemize}

In order for our project to function two basic needs need to be met:
\begin{itemize}
 \item There needs to be a demand for our services
 \item We need to have resources to host our product to allow users to utilize it.
\end{itemize}

Without these Basic Needs we will either be unable to supply our product to the consumer base or we will be wasting our resources for a product that generates no return.

\subsubsection{INPUT}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Resources:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Financial:} Initial investments for salaries and server hosting.
        \item \textbf{Human Resources:} 1 Frontend Developer, 1 Backend Developer, 2 Full-Stack Developers.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User Data Collection}
    \begin{itemize}
        
        \item \textbf{Consistency:} The application counts users' \label{sec:activeDays}\textbf{active days} - at least one login and one game played. A \label{sec:consistencyCoef}\textbf{consistency coefficient} is calculated which resets every month:
        \begin{equation*}
            \text{Consistency coefficient} = \frac{\text{Active days this month}}{\text{Passed days of this month}}
        \end{equation*}
        \item \textbf{Time Spent:} Total playtime recorded, inspired by Steam profiles (e.g., last two weeks of activity). At the start of a game a timer will start and at the end it will stop. Then it will add up on the time spent playing games today and also this week.
        \item \textbf{Popularity Metrics:} Application tracks the number of users that are logged in and playing games. Inspired by Steam Charts\label{sec:steamCharts}: active users now, peak users today (24-hour peak), all-time users peak.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Marketing \& Revenue Generation:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Marketing Company:} Third-party ad services dynamically assign ads based on user activity (e.g., Google AdSense). "We've seen a substantial revenue increase and a significant reduction in manual effort thanks to Auto ads in-page formats, while sustaining user engagement and retention. — Yangyang Xi, CEO of Lang-8, Inc."\cite{AutoAds}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{PROCESS}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Preparation}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Application/Game Development:} Programmers create/improve the application and interactive reaction-based games.
        \item \textbf{Account creation:} Users sign up.
        \item \textbf{Personalized Ad Assignment:} User activity is tracked via cookies, and ads are assigned via third-party marketing companies.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Gaming}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Maintenance}: Bug Fixes \& Updates. Developers ensure smooth functionality and release of new games/features.
        \item \textbf{User Interaction:} Users play games and see personalized ads.
        \item \textbf{Ad Delivery \& Revenue Tracking:} The marketing company serves ads and records performance.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Data Tracking \& Analytics}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{User Progress Tracking:} The application logs \label{sec:reactionScores}\textbf{reaction scores} - the longer the user survives in a game and the higher level he reaches - the higher score he gets. The application will also log users improvement trends. User will be able to see if he has improved by compare his scores in leaderboards and past results.
        \item \textbf{Leaderboard System:} Users are ranked based on performance over time and todays highest results. Leaderboard for ongoing month, highest scoring players. Leaderboard today - todays highest scoring users.
        \item \textbf{Ad Analytics:} Ad views and clicks are analyzed to optimize revenue (Marketing company job).
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{OUTPUT}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Short-Term:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{User Engagement \& Satisfaction:} Measured via \textbf{Consistency Coefficient} - closer to 1 indicates higher user cosistency.
        \item \textbf{Growing User Base:} Increasing active user counts and time spent on games (Growing popularity metrics).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Long-Term:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Improved Reaction Time:} Users' reaction scores should improve over time, with leaderboard rankings reflecting progress.
        \item \textbf{Ad Revenue:} Funds ongoing development and investor returns.
        \item \textbf{Potential Premium Features:} Subscription model, ad-free experience, or additional analytics for users.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Entity relation diagram}
The entity relation diagram models the entities and their relationships in the system. The following figure shows the entity relation diagram of the Human Benchmark system.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{images/DB-ClassDiagram.drawio.png}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{User entity} - Contains user information such as name, email, and password. User can create games and play games. Scores are shown in the leaderboard.
    \item \textbf{Game entity} - Contains game information such as name, description, target count, difficulty level, type. Users can create games and play games. Games also track how many targets the user has hit and missed, and the user's score based on that.
    \item \textbf{Target entity} - Contains target information such as speed and size. Targets are part of the game. Targets appear on the screen and users have to click on them. After the set duration expires, the target disappears.
    \item \textbf{Score entity} - Contains score information for users. Scores are shown in the leaderboard for each game.
    \item \textbf{Game session entity} - Contains game session information such as start and end time. Game sessions are created when users play games.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Requirements}
\label{sec:requirements}
This section will discuss the user needs and requirements of the Human Benchmark system.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{User must be able to view their high scores}  Currently the system provides no means by which they can check their own progress in a certain game. To counteract this for our users we will add the ability to view each players high score for each game after the game is concluded. As such users will be able to see whether or not they are improving or not. It is important for the business company, our stakeholders, because they wand to attract more users to gain more profits via advertising. As such they have expressed it as a need, which we will implement, so that or product will offer a better experience for the users. This will be implemented by the software engineer team. The team will use Scrum as a way to ensure constant development and communication will occour during the beginning of the sprint when the team will discuss what changes need to be made, communicate, etc. The use case for this feature is simple. After playing a game the screen will not only display the score that was achieved during the session, but also the top score that that user has ever achieved. Maintenance for this requirement will be virtually not required since it will not interfere with any of the core functionality. To implement this we will add new entries in a database that will store the User, The game and the top score that has been achieved. Upon finishing the game this value will be retrieved and, if it has improved, updated accordingly.

 \item \textbf{Private games must be implemented}   Our product currently only allows to play 'public' games i.e. if someone creates a game anyone has the ability to join the session. To fix this we will add private games - games which don't appear in the public list and where only select players can join. This is also important for our stakeholders, because it would make our product more appealing to users and atract more of them from competators. They have expressed a need to add such games, so that users may, if they choose to, have private games so that they could control with who they can compete: for instance play with friends, family members, etc. This will also be implemented by our software engineer team and will follow the same methods in regards to the development and communication proccess. Maintenance will not be a large drawback, it will not affect the game itself only the connection. The use case is as follows: a user, when selecting a game may choose, via a checkbox, to have a private game, after which a new window will open showing the currently waiting players and a code, by which they can join the private game. Simmilarly, there will also be a standalone page in which a user may enter a code to join a private game. Once that is completed, the host(player that created the private game) may begin the game via click of a button upon which the game will commence and no new plyers will be allowed to join. This will be implemented by adding entries of private games storing the game, host, code, connected players, and "joinability". Upon the start of the game the joinability of the game will be set to false as to not allow new players to join.

 \item \textbf{A game history must be implemented} Currently there is no way to see the history of the games that a logged in user has played. The business company has expressed a need to have game history so that our app users can better track their progress. This task will also be carried out by the development team(Software Engineers) with the same methods for dialog and development. Maintenance will be small since it won't conflict with the main aspects of our product. The use case is as follows: when a user opens their profile, they will see a list which will contain all of the games that they have played which will include their score, the time of completion and what kind of game it was. This requirement will be achieved by adding new entries in the database which will have entries with the aforementioned data and a user.

 \item \textbf{A rating system for user-created games must be implemented} By the current structure and functionality of our system, there is no way to rate games. This is important because there is no obvious way to determine the quality of the game, as such the stakeholders have expressed it as a need. This task will as well be caried out by our development team with the same methodology. The use case is as folows: When opening the page for a game a running total of "rates" will be shown. A logged in user then has the choice to either increase it by one or decrease it by one as such rating the game. Along with the absolute total, the site will also display a relative "rating" calculated as the ratio of those who liked it(chose to increase the rating) and those who didn't (decreaced the rating). This will be implemented by adding a new field to the database with the total number of "ups" and "Downs" and this data will be retrieved, shown and calculated when the game is opened. The expected maintenence is small since no changes to the games itself will take place.

 \item \textbf{Users must be able to view the top 10 players for each game} Currently the product not only doesn't have a way to view the highest score of a specific user but also in general. As such the business company have made a request to add it. Simmilarly to the other requirements it will be implemented by the developer team and following the same methods. The use-case is as such: unlike the user's top score which will be diplayed after the game has ended; the "global" top score will be shown before as a list of usernames and their respective scores. The amount of entries in this list will be limited to 10 as to not clutter the screen and provide better user experience. This will be implemented in a simmilar manner to the requirement mentioned before, new entries added in the database to save these values. But unlike the previous requirement, since this data is subject to frequent change, a new entrie will be created that will uniquely store The top ranked players in each game.
\end{itemize}
